2023 · Album

ARTISAN

Making of

As a psychoanalyst and as a producer, I find myself asking what it even means to produce music today. I’ll keep it short, but it’s worth talking about.

Over the last 15 years, production has become widely democratized — the means to make a good record have grown more accessible and affordable, within reach of communities that until recently were priced out.

Paradoxically, the more accessible production has become, the more violently independent venues — those not affiliated with any group in power — have been opposed. Opposed by the market that bought them up, by the decorum and public quiet that silenced them, by the release-consume-die logic of the platforms that commodified them, by the pandemic that shut them down, and by governments’ phobia of so-called young people, feared and belittled whenever they’re not simply consumers.

Independent spaces mean, above all, places that bring people together — that create a sense of belonging, and with it, a reason to want to stay alive.

Another paradox: the more music production has grown in sheer numbers, the more uniform it has become, sonically and culturally — de-socialized from context. You could ask how much of today’s music production actually helps create contexts that bring people together, and how much instead pulls them apart.

This record is fed by those reflections, and wants to be a pretext to meet — to just connect. I wanted to make a record that was “handmade,” to be danced to and heard through a good sound system. I thought about radicalizing the artisanal — if you like, “local” — approach to sound. So I produced and mixed it at my desk at home, in Pigneto, weaving together samples from vinyl records God had forgotten and the sounds of the synth my parents gave me in high school. So this is it: just connect.
